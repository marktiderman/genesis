Observability: Sentry from Genesis settings, with no build. `observabilitySettings` declares `observability.enabled`, `observability.sentryDsn` (checked against `SENTRY_DSN_PATTERN`) and `observability.sentryOrg`, also in package.json `genesis.settings` (now a list: devtools, observability). `sentrySink` returns a `SentrySink`: `applySettings(settings)` starts Sentry live from the settings' DSN when the build has none, closes it live when `observability.enabled` turns off: its hooks drop everything from that moment, the SDK's own captures included, and `SentryLike.close` then releases the client, and leaves a new DSN, or turning it back on, to the next launch (`status().restartPending`); `cache` (AsyncStorage, or localStorage) keeps the settings' values so later launches start at once; `healthCheck()` fits genesis-support's `healthChecks`. A `Sentry.init` that throws when the settings start it leaves Sentry off and is reported there (`status().problem`), never thrown into the settings watch. The build's `EXPO_PUBLIC_SENTRY_DSN` still wins. Also `resolveSentryDsn`, `parseSentryDsn`, `sentryDsnProblem` and `observabilitySettingValues`.

Changed: without a DSN, the sink now always has its methods, and they do nothing while Sentry isn't running (it was a sink with none). The missing-DSN message is now a development-only notice, printed once when the settings have been read and give no DSN either; it was a `console.warn` outside development at start-up. `onMissingDsn` still replaces it, in any build.
